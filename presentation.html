● CLINICAL MOTIVATION & DILEMMA

The Dual Challenge: Silent Decline vs. Data Silos

Why traditional clinical early warning scoring and cloud AI architectures fail in acute care.

⚠️ The Bedside Crisis
  • Silent Physiological Drift: Deterioration develops over 4–12 hours through subtle cross-vital covariance (elevating heart rate, dropping blood pressure) before acute collapse.
  • NEWS2 Threshold Limitations: Static rule-based scoring (NEWS2 ≥ 5) creates severe alert fatigue with high false alarm rates.
  • Zero Forecasting Lead-Time: Scoring current vitals only flags deterioration when it is already happening, leaving zero time for prophylactic fluid resuscitation.
🔒 The Privacy & Governance Roadblock
  • Strict Data Silos (HIPAA / GDPR): Patient telemetry cannot be pooled into centralized cloud servers or vendor databases.
  • Gradient Reconstruction Attacks: Standard deep learning models inadvertently memorize individual patient outliers during backprop.
  • Cold Risk Scores are Unusable: Handing a nurse an alert probability of 0.82 does not explain why the patient is deteriorating or what interventions are needed.
Talking Points (60s): "Patient deterioration is almost never sudden; it is preceded by hours of subtle multi-vital drift. Standard scoring tools like NHS NEWS2 trigger massive alert fatigue and only react when collapse has already begun. Machine learning can forecast hours ahead, but regulations forbid pooling ICU records. Furthermore, giving a doctor an unexplained risk score of 0.82 is clinically useless. We need early forecasting, mathematical privacy guarantees, and verifiable natural language explanations."

● DATASET & TASK FORMULATION

PhysioNet 2019: 40,336 Real ICU Stays

Zero data leakage, authentic imputation accounting, and 4–6 hour deterioration forecasting.

PARTITION Patient-Level Splits
  • Strict ID Splitting: 80/20 train/test split strictly by patient ID, never by window. Zero leakage.
  • Two Hospital Silos: Site A (20,336 ICU stays) and Site B (20,000 ICU stays).
  • Cryptographic Freeze: SHA-256 manifest locks all numpy arrays before training.
WINDOW 12h Stream + 4–6h Horizon
  • Input Tensor: $X \in \mathbb{R}^{12 \times 6}$ (HR, SBP, O2Sat, Resp, Temp, Glucose).
  • Target Formulation: $\max(\text{NEWS2}_{t+4 \dots t+6}) \ge 5$.
  • Why 4–6 Hours: Predicting $t=0$ is trivial autocorrelation. Forecasting 4–6h ahead gives clinicians actionable intervention lead-time.
RIGOR Documented Imputation
  • Forward-Fill with Report: Forward then backward-fill. We explicitly measure missingness:
  • HR: 9.9% | O2Sat: 12.8%
  • SBP: 15.2% | Resp: 15.8%
  • Temp: 66.8% | Glucose: 83.2%
Talking Points (60s): "We use PhysioNet 2019 — 40,336 real ICU patients across two hospital systems. We rejected Synthea because synthetic vitals lacked continuous hourly timestamps and missed temperature and oxygen data. We enforce patient-level splitting — no sliding windows overlap between train and test. Notice our prediction horizon: we forecast the maximum NEWS2 score 4 to 6 hours into the future. Predicting zero hours ahead is trivial because the label is derived from the vitals you're looking at; 4 to 6 hours is the clinically meaningful problem."

● RESEARCH INSIGHT (CONTRIBUTION C2)

Why Recurrent Models Tolerate Privacy Noise

Recurrent gating acts as an intrinsic low-pass filter against Opacus DP-SGD gradient noise.

⚡ DPLSTM & DPGRU (Recurrent)
  • Temporal State Persistence: Hidden and cell states preserve macro trend directions across timesteps.
  • Low-Pass Noise Filtering: Forget and reset gates naturally attenuate high-frequency Gaussian gradient perturbation injected during DP-SGD.
  • GroupNorm Stabilization: Replacing BatchNorm with GroupNorm prevents cross-sample information leakage while maintaining recurrent convergence.
📉 CNN1D (Convolutional)
  • Fragile Local Receptive Fields: 1D convolutional kernels operate across fixed temporal patches without recurrent memory.
  • Gradient Noise Vulnerability: Adding Gaussian noise directly disrupts localized kernel activations, causing steeper accuracy decay under ε ≤ 2.0.
  • Confirmed Hypothesis: Confirms Contribution C2 — sequential recurrence is superior for private clinical time-series.
Talking Points (45s): "Why do LSTM and GRU beat CNNs under differential privacy? In DP-SGD, we clip gradients and inject Gaussian noise. Recurrent networks have forget and reset gates that accumulate state over 12 hours, acting like low-pass filters that smooth out that perturbation. Convolutional kernels have rigid local receptive fields — noise directly warps their feature detectors. This directly validates our Contribution C2."
● TRACK B — WHAT THE RESEARCH ESTABLISHED

Research Track Conclusion

Four findings from the 60-epoch federated sweep, and the two limits we are explicit about.

FINDING 1 Privacy is affordable here
At the gold-standard budget ε = 1.0, DPLSTM reaches 0.885 AUROC / 0.719 AUPRC, catching 86.4% of deteriorations 4–6h ahead at F1 = 0.808. The guarantee is formal — a bounded, accounted ε, not a promise.
FINDING 2 Recurrence beats convolution under noise
DPLSTM and DPGRU hold 0.86–0.90 AUROC across the whole ε range. CNN1D collapses — at ε = 4.0 it misses every deterioration (FNR = 1.000). Recurrent gates low-pass-filter DP-SGD noise; fixed kernels have no equivalent defence. C2 confirmed.
FINDING 3 The threshold matters more than the budget
ε = 2.0 ranks best (0.898 AUROC) but at a fixed τ = 0.5 it fires on nothing. Ranking quality and clinical utility are different axes: the operating point must be calibrated per budget, so we ship a calibrated τ = 0.385 rather than a default.
FINDING 4 Federation held across sites
Sample-weighted FedAvg across Site A and B converged without raw data movement, and the Rényi accountant — persisted across rounds rather than silently reset — kept the reported ε truthful for the whole run, not just the last round.
⚠ Stated limits — (1) our non-DP baseline scores below its own DP runs (0.824 vs 0.885 AUROC); we read clipping and noise as regularisation on a small, imbalanced cohort, and report it as an anomaly to investigate rather than as evidence that privacy improves accuracy. (2) The two sites are simulated from one PhysioNet cohort, so cross-institution heterogeneity is modelled, not observed.
Talking Points (60s): "So what does the research track actually conclude? Four things. One: at epsilon 1.0 we keep 0.885 AUROC and catch 86.4% of deteriorations four to six hours early — privacy is affordable on this problem. Two: recurrence beats convolution under DP noise, and the CNN baseline collapses completely — that confirms Contribution C2. Three: the alert threshold matters more than the privacy budget; epsilon 2.0 ranks best but fires on nothing at a default threshold, so we ship a calibrated tau of 0.385. Four: federation converged across both sites with a continuous privacy accountant. And we are explicit about two limits: our non-DP baseline scored lower than its DP runs, which we report as an anomaly rather than spin, and our two sites are simulated from one cohort."

Talking Points (75s): "This is the mechanism, and it's the heart of the contribution. A reading arrives and joins a rolling twelve-hour window. We score it twice — the NEWS2 rule and the private detector — and raise an alert if either crosses. Now the important step: we extract exactly which vitals breached and by how much, and we build the retrieval query out of those specific abnormalities. That's the junction. It is not a generic patient lookup; the alert's own content becomes the search. Retrieval is filtered on patient ID before the model sees a single token. Then the local model writes one paragraph. Look at the split on the right: everything numeric is computed, and only the prose is generated. That's why a missed vital is impossible by construction rather than something we hope the model complies with."
● CLINICAL PRODUCT (TRACK A)

WardSense: Clinician Decision Workstation

Bedside telemetry replay, strict per-patient ChromaDB isolation, and offline LLM inference.

WARD Triage board
Six beds as a ruled list ordered by detector risk, not a grid of equal tiles. Ordering is itself the information: the answer to “who is deteriorating” is the top row.
BEDSIDE Trajectories
Four vitals as small multiples with normal-range bands, NEWS2 on its own axis with the threshold drawn, and detector risk as a bullet against its calibrated τ. Out-of-range points are marked by shape and colour and label.
REPLAY Telemetry engine
Play, pause, scrub and step a recorded stay hour by hour at 0.25–2s per ICU hour. Labelled REPLAY everywhere — a recorded PhysioNet stay, never claimed as a live feed.
DEMO Guided walkthrough
A scripted mode that runs a scenario as numbered chapters. Each names one panel, rings it, dims the rest, and states in a sentence what to watch for.
C3 Alert→summary bridge
Deterministic breach table plus a grounded narrative, with the synthesised retrieval query and every retrieved chunk shown as inspectable evidence.
ASK Grounded Q&A
Free-text questions answered from one record. The patient_id filter is applied at query time, and an adversarial isolation test asserts cross-patient retrieval returns empty, not merely different.
ASSURANCE Provenance page
Privacy posture and evaluation state, with every figure read from the evaluation artifact at render time and withheld when its provenance cannot be vouched for.
PORTAL Patient view
The same retrieval boundary exposed to the patient, with an explicit information-only disclaimer: it explains what is recorded, and never diagnoses or prescribes.
Talking Points (60s): "Track A is the workstation, and these are the features that exist and run. The ward view is a triage list ordered by risk, because a ward board's job is to answer who is deteriorating — six identical tiles throw that ordering away. The bedside view puts the trajectories next to the alert that they caused. The replay engine drives it hour by hour, and it is labelled replay everywhere — we are not claiming a live hospital feed. Demo mode is a scripted walkthrough that tells you which panel to look at. Then the C3 bridge, grounded Q&A with a hard patient filter backed by an adversarial test, an assurance page that withholds figures it cannot vouch for, and a patient-facing portal on the same boundary."
● CLINICAL VERIFICATION & PROVENANCE

How We Verify — and What We Refuse to Claim

Three evaluation layers over 20 held-out scenarios, and the gate that decides whether their numbers may be shown.

LAYER 1 Programmatic fact check
Claim-level extraction against a per-patient ground-truth fact list. Deliberately not keyword matching — we proved keyword checkers pass fabricated content that never trips a watchlist word.
LAYER 2 Clinical rubric
Five dimensions — accuracy, relevance, completeness, safety, conciseness — scored per scenario, with Cohen's κ reported between raters rather than averaged away.
LAYER 3 LLM-as-judge
The same rubric applied by a judge model, kept as a third independent signal so disagreement between the layers is visible instead of hidden.
🔍 The provenance gate
Every figure on the Assurance page is read from stage8_evaluation_report.json at render time — nothing is hardcoded, and a CI test fails the build if an evaluation number is ever pasted into the UI.

The page also checks whether that artifact came from a real model run. Identical narratives across two models, or constant latency, means template-derived — and the page says so and withholds the numbers.
⚠ Current status: withheld
Our current artifact fails that gate: it was generated while the model server was unreachable, so the offline fallback wrote every narrative — both models returned byte-identical text.

So we are not quoting a hallucination rate, a κ, or a 3B-vs-8B recommendation today. The harness is built and tested; the numbers land once it re-runs live. We would rather show the gate working than a figure we cannot defend.
Talking Points (75s): "Evaluation is three layers: programmatic claim-level fact checking against ground truth, a five-dimension clinical rubric with Cohen's kappa, and an LLM judge as an independent third signal. Now the part I want to be straight about. Every figure on our Assurance page is read from the evaluation artifact at render time — nothing hardcoded, and a CI test fails the build if anyone pastes a number into the UI. The page also checks the artifact's provenance: if two different models returned identical narratives, or latency is constant across every scenario, that artifact came from the offline fallback path, not a model. Our current artifact fails that check — it was generated while the model server was down. So we are deliberately not quoting a hallucination rate or a kappa today. The harness is built and tested; we re-run it against a live model and the page fills itself in. I would rather show you the gate working than quote a number I can't defend under questioning."
